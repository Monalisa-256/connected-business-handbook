# People and administration: useful help with careful boundaries

## Case A — Staff handbook questions

An employee asks: "How do I request annual leave?"

A policy assistant looks up the current handbook, provides the relevant procedure, and links to the approved form or policy page. It should not claim to approve leave or interpret a dispute as legal advice.

A good answer identifies the exact policy version and says when the case should be referred to HR.

## Case B — New employee onboarding

When a manager records that someone is joining, an approved workflow can create an onboarding checklist: orientation, training material, equipment request, and introductions. Account provisioning and access rights should follow an organization's identity and security procedures. The assistant should not grant system-admin access based only on an email request.

## Case C — Interview scheduling

The assistant may read authorized interviewer calendars, propose times, and prepare scheduling messages. It must not leak private calendar details to candidates. Decisions about hiring or ranking applicants should remain with accountable people and be reviewed for fairness and applicable legal requirements.

## Case D — Meeting notes and follow-up

With participants' knowledge and appropriate recording consent, a transcript or set of approved notes can be turned into an action list. The owner of the meeting confirms which decisions were actually made. People should be able to correct an inaccurate action item.

## Case E — A request to change payroll details

Treat bank details, salary changes, and identity information as sensitive. Route them through the company's verified HR/payroll process. Do not act on a document or message alone.

## Suggested first project

Create a **read-only policy assistant** using the current handbook. Test ambiguous questions and outdated policy versions. Limit it to pointing employees to correct procedures and to the person responsible for exceptions.

## Principle worth keeping

Staff need clear avenues to contest an incorrect result, especially where the result could affect pay, work opportunities, leave, or disciplinary matters. A confident machine-generated response is not a substitute for the company's accountable decision-maker.

## Why internal staff work deserves the same caution as customer work

An internal assistant may answer general leave policy questions, prepare onboarding checklists and schedule interviews. But employee files often contain sensitive information that should not be searchable by every staff member. Payroll changes, disciplinary decisions, medical information, hiring judgments and access grants require especially careful role boundaries.

For an onboarding request, the assistant might prepare a checklist and notify the relevant departments. Actual system access should follow an approval process and be granted by the authorized administrator. Removing access when employment ends is equally important; an old connected account can remain active after the person has left.

### Example of a useful response

> The current staff handbook explains the leave application steps and points to the approved form. I can help prepare the request. An authorized manager or HR representative will review eligibility and approval; I cannot decide your leave balance from this message.
