# Business example library

Each example begins with something an owner or customer would actually say. The same underlying approach can be reused across many industries: identify the request, find approved information, act within permission limits, and record the outcome.

## How to read a scenario

Every case describes the request, the relevant records, the assistants involved, how the work should proceed, where a human must intervene, and what counts as completion. It also names common failure modes so you can test the system instead of trusting a demonstration.

**Important:** The names, prices, orders and dates in these scenarios are invented teaching examples. They do not represent live inventory, customer records, quotations, or bookings.

## How to turn an example into a real process

First identify the business event and what a finished job means. Then locate the authoritative record. Decide who can read it, who may create a draft and who must authorize a change. Write at least one example where information is missing or contradictory. Only then choose the model and builder.

The [full case-study library](../09-case-studies/01-retail-voice-photo-sales.md) expands common situations into sample conversations, records, diagrams, approvals and test cases. These examples can be adapted to another industry, but the same tool should not inherit permissions from unrelated departments simply because the flow looks similar.

**Useful rule:** Copy the *process pattern*, not another business's sensitive data or financial decision rules.
