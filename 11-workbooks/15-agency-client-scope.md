# Workbook: scoping a workforce project for a client

A consultant can provide useful setup and support, but should not sell broad "autonomous employees" without specifying exactly what they do. This worksheet creates a clear agreement between the person configuring the system and the business that owns it.

## A usable statement of work

Define the **in-scope workflow** and its input channels. Name the exact connected systems. State whether the delivered system only drafts, creates tasks or sends messages. List the approvals, source documents, test cases, acceptance criteria, maintenance period and support response process. Treat new channels or financial actions as separate changes requiring fresh review.

### Example: two different quotes

**Poor offer:** "We install an AI employee that runs your company."  
**Clear offer:** "We configure a supervised email-intake workflow that classifies incoming service requests, checks the approved service guide, prepares a draft reply and logs a task. Sending remains with your nominated manager. We will test it against 30 agreed sample cases and provide a permission register and handover session."

## Handover and exit

The client should control the business accounts, records and invoices. Document how to export workflows where supported, rotate or revoke credentials, transfer administrative ownership, preserve required logs and close paid services. Keep client-specific material out of the consultant's general reusable templates.
