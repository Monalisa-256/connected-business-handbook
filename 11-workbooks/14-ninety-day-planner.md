# Workbook: plan a 90-day rollout by evidence, not enthusiasm

A new stage begins only after the previous stage meets its exit criteria. The dates are planning markers; integration, legal review or poor data can extend the timeline.

| Period | Main work | Deliverable | Exit decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1–15 | Select process and gather verified sources | Process map, owner, baseline | Feasible and worth testing? |
| Days 16–30 | Build narrow agent/workflow and permissions | Working demo with logs | Forbidden actions blocked? |
| Days 31–45 | Test normal, difficult and hostile cases | Evaluation record | Quality acceptable for a pilot? |
| Days 46–60 | Limited live pilot with review | Verified completion and cost data | Benefits greater than problems? |
| Days 61–75 | Correct recurring failures; add one media type | Revised approved workflow | Improvements verified? |
| Days 76–90 | Expand cautiously or stop | Operations plan and next budget | Owner approval recorded? |

## The stage-gate questions

1. Does every task have an assigned person or specialist?
2. Are record lookups current and access-controlled?
3. Can a manager see the difference between a draft and a completed action?
4. Do approval controls still work after retries and handoffs?
5. Does the workflow actually improve service, cost or accuracy compared with the old process?
6. Can the business shut it down without losing track of customer requests?
