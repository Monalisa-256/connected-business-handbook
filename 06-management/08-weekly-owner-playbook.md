# The owner's weekly operating routine

Installing a workflow is the beginning of an operating responsibility, not the end. If nobody checks the results, small errors become habits and expired connections turn into missed customer work.

## Daily: ten minutes of exception review

Open the task register and answer:

- Which items are still waiting for information?
- Which items have no owner?
- Which customer-facing actions are waiting for approval?
- Which tool calls failed or returned an uncertain result?
- Are any tasks overdue or apparently duplicated?

The goal is not to read every successful transcript. It is to make unfinished work visible and assign it.

## Weekly: a review with the people doing the job

Choose a small sample of completed tasks. Compare the result against the original request and real business records. Ask staff whether the workflow saved time or created correction work. Look for patterns rather than isolated anecdotes.

### Example weekly meeting agenda

| Minutes | Discussion |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Any urgent or sensitive failures? |
| 5–10 | Number of requests completed and still open |
| 10–20 | Review five recent cases, including at least one difficult case |
| 20–25 | Which source, rule or permission needs correction? |
| 25–30 | Assign improvements and retesting owner |

## Monthly: decide whether to keep, change or stop

Review subscription and usage costs, human correction hours, customer outcomes, and current vendor permissions. Check whether an old employee or supplier still controls any connection. Re-run the most important tests after product updates.

## A simple change record

| Date | What changed? | Reason | Tests repeated | Approved by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [ ] | [New price-list source] | [Old price appeared] | [T-03, T-05] | [ ] |

## When a mistake is reported

1. Protect the customer or business from further harm.
2. Stop the risky action or disconnect the tool if necessary.
3. Record the affected task and confirmed facts.
4. Correct the actual source, workflow or permission problem.
5. Repeat the relevant tests before restoring operation.
6. Tell affected staff or customers what is appropriate under company procedures.

## Train staff to challenge results

Employees should feel free to say, "That stock figure is old," or "This customer has not been verified." A workflow that people cannot question is difficult to improve. Give staff a clear route for corrections and urgent handovers.

## A sensible longer-term ambition

After several reliable workflows are running, the business can coordinate them under a shared intake and task register. It should still be possible for an owner to open any task and answer: **Who requested it, what happened, which records were checked, and what is the next step?**

## Sample Monday review: the customer-support queue

A small distributor processed 86 customer inquiries last week. Fourteen required staff attention. The dashboard shows three inquiries waiting for product availability, two unanswered complaints and one duplicate CRM record. Instead of asking the team merely “is the automation working?”, the owner asks for the task IDs and the last verified action on each.

The operations manager owns the availability questions. The service lead owns the complaints. The workflow maintainer investigates the duplicate record, checks whether a repeated trigger produced it, and designs a test before changing the process.

### Weekly decision record

| Decision | Evidence | Owner | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep draft-only replies for refunds | Three cases lacked verified order history | Service manager | Next Friday |
| Correct one obsolete price document | Two quotations used outdated reference | Sales manager | Wednesday |
| Add duplicate-trigger test | CRM produced duplicate opportunity | Workflow maintainer | Thursday |
| Continue pilot for another week | No critical breaches but unresolved backlog | Business owner | Next Monday |

### What staff should bring to the meeting

A short list of failed or delayed tasks, any approval requests that were skipped or misunderstood, a few customer messages illustrating quality problems, current platform costs, and the changes made since the last review. Avoid replacing that evidence with a slide full of generic success percentages.

Once a month, revisit permissions, the set of available tools, document owners, and vendor changes. When a key staff member leaves, verify that their accounts and approvals were not silently embedded in the workflow.
