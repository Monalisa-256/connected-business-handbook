# What will it cost, and is it worth it?

The cheapest model is not always the cheapest business process. A fast model that produces many errors may cause expensive staff corrections. A premium model may be unnecessary for a simple classification task.

## Where the money goes

| Cost | What it may cover |
|---|---|
| Main platform | Subscription, seats, workflow runs or credits |
| Model use | Reading, reasoning, drafting and tool calls |
| Speech | Voice minutes, transcription and voice generation |
| Documents | Pages or files processed |
| Creative media | Images, design or video generation |
| App connections | Premium integrations or provider usage |
| Business records | Storage, database or CRM charges |
| Human time | Review, correction, supervision and maintenance |

Check current published pricing and your own plan. This handbook intentionally avoids fixed price claims that could become outdated.

## Worked example: an internal email-sorting process

Suppose a staff member currently spends 30 minutes each working day sorting incoming requests. Assume 20 working days in a month and an estimated fully loaded staff cost of 12 currency units per hour.

- Existing monthly effort: 0.5 hours × 20 days = **10 hours**.
- Estimated equivalent cost: 10 × 12 = **120 currency units**.
- Pilot platform and model usage: **40 units** per month (illustrative, not vendor pricing).
- Remaining human review: **3 hours** × 12 = **36 units**.
- Total proposed monthly cost: 40 + 36 = **76 units**.
- Illustrative net monthly saving: 120 − 76 = **44 units**, **if** accuracy, quality and workload assumptions hold.

This calculation ignores one-time setup and other possible effects. Add those in an actual decision.

## The right denominator

Measure **cost per correctly completed task**, not simply number of messages processed.

If 100 requests run but 20 must be completely redone, count the rework cost and the business consequences of mistakes.

## When to stop a project

If the workflow is rarely used, repeatedly needs complete human correction, relies on poor data, or creates risks that outweigh the savings, simplify or retire it. A successful pilot may be the discovery that existing software and a clearer office procedure are enough.

## When to expand

Expand after several weeks of reliable results, a clear owner, manageable operating cost, stable connections, and evidence that customers or staff actually benefit.

## A more complete decision: 1,000 requests per month

Consider a fictional business processing 1,000 routine inquiries. At present, staff spend an average of eight minutes on each: roughly 133.3 staff hours. At a fully loaded cost of 18 currency units per hour, that is about **2,400 units** of allocated labor. This is not automatically cash that can be removed from payroll; it is a way of valuing the capacity used.

A proposed workflow costs 250 units in platform charges, 110 units for model and specialist processing, and 40 units for records and integrations. Staff still spend 30 hours reviewing exceptions and maintaining the process, or 540 units at the same hourly rate. Total estimated recurring cost is **940 units**. If all 1,000 requests are completed at acceptable quality, the indicative capacity value is **1,460 units per month** before setup costs, opportunity costs, and uncertainty.

But what if 150 requests are mishandled and each needs 12 more minutes of rework? That adds 30 staff hours and 540 units, reducing the indicative net benefit to **920 units**. If those errors damage sales or customers' trust, the true cost may be much higher. This is why cost per *correct* completed task is the important measure.

### Costs owners frequently leave out

- Staff time preparing clean reference documents and keeping them current.
- Testing after model, connector and workflow updates.
- Telephony minutes, transcription, video and document processing charges.
- Unsuccessful runs and repeated attempts after outages.
- Approval time, escalations and customer correction messages.
- Extra seats or premium connectors required by specific integrations.
- One-time setup costs, training, and any consultant support.

### Decision memo

Record the baseline, the new measured completion rate, actual review hours, recurring bills, setup expense, and confidence level in the figures. Keep benefits such as faster response and reduced backlog separately from cash savings unless the financial effect can be shown.
