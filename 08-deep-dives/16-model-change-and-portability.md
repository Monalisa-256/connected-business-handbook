# Avoiding lock-in: keep the business portable

Models and platforms change rapidly. The strongest long-term asset is not a particular assistant's wording; it is the company's documented process, verified data, permissions, tests, and evidence of completed work.

## What should belong to the business

Keep copies of approved company policies, task definitions, role descriptions, normal test cases, decision records and important output artifacts under business ownership. Wherever possible, store customer, order and financial records in recognized systems of record rather than only inside a proprietary agent chat history.

## Six ways a provider change can hurt

1. Model version retirement changes the wording or reliability of responses.
2. A connector changes its available tools, causing a previously working workflow to fail.
3. A plan change removes a needed feature or alters usage charges.
4. The provider adds new data-retention or processing terms that require review.
5. A platform export omits hidden configuration or detailed run history.
6. A business account is tied to one employee who later leaves.

## The portability pack

| Asset | Keep this record | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow map | Diagram and exact acceptance criteria | Rebuild elsewhere |
| Role instructions | Approved version and owner | Preserve behavior requirements |
| Business documents | Original approved files | Avoid losing knowledge |
| Tool permissions | Connection register and account owner | Recreate safe access |
| Test cases | Inputs, expected outputs, critical failures | Compare replacement provider |
| Task history | Open task IDs and statuses | Prevent lost work |
| Vendor terms | Contract, data export process, support rules | Manage exit responsibly |

## A controlled model switch

Run the candidate model on the same representative test set, compare critical errors, check language and modality support, review data terms, validate tool calls with limited permissions, and deploy first to a small pilot group. Do not replace a model on Friday evening simply because a new version was announced.

## A controlled platform exit

Before disconnecting, export records using supported options, identify pending tasks, preserve required logs, assign an owner to each open case, revoke tokens or authorizations, and verify that automated triggers no longer run. Confirm who owns the files and contact channels after the service stops.

**Owner's conclusion:** Buy flexibility where it matters, but do not pay for elaborate portability features before there is a real risk or scale to justify them.
